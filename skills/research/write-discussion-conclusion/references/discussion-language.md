# Discussion language bank

## Relating the study to existing work

| Relation | Wording |
| --- | --- |
| Agrees | consistent with, in line with, in good agreement with, supports, corroborates, lends support to |
| Extends | extends, broadens, expands, builds on |
| Refines | refines, modifies, qualifies |
| Disagrees | contrasts with, differs from, challenges, contradicts, does not support |
| Different route | offers an alternative approach to, uses a different method to reach similar results |
| New | to our knowledge, the first to report (after a thorough search) |

A simple comparative also works: "faster than", "less sensitive to". Name the compared work and attribute precisely.

## Stating achievement

Present perfect or present simple: "We have shown", "This study provides", "These results reveal". Match intensity to evidence.

- Strong evidence: striking, remarkable, unexpected, dramatic, clear, compelling.
- Moderate: useful, effective, accurate, reliable, practical, robust.
- Modest: encouraging, promising, preliminary.

Do not use exclamation marks; precise strong words carry enthusiasm. If the work is early-stage, say so.

## Explaining and interpreting

"One explanation is", "This may reflect", "A possible mechanism is", "Alternatively", "This could also arise from". Offer at least one rival explanation when the evidence allows more than one.

## Limitations and future work

- Pair the limitation with a positive result that stands: "Although the hypothesis was supported in this cohort, retention was not reassessed."
- Invitation: "should be replicated", "remains to be determined", "is worth exploring", "further work is needed to".
- Own plan: "We will", "we are currently". Do not use these for ideas you are not pursuing.

## Applications

Use modals unless the application was tested: "could be used for", "may eventually lead to", "has potential in". Match the claimed domain to the tested domain.

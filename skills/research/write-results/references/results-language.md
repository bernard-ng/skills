# Results language bank

Use only wording that the data support.

## Looking back

"As noted in the Introduction", "To test this prediction", "Since X is critical, we examined", "As described above", "In our study".

## Overview

"In most cases", "overall", "on the whole", "in general", "the response was", "the results fall into two parts". Add the exception when there is one.

## Inviting the reader to a display

Verbs: shows, presents, summarises, lists, plots, compares, illustrates, reports, gives. A table lists or summarises; a graph plots or shows. "As can be seen from Fig. 2" means deduced from the figure; "in Fig. 2" means it appears there.

## Objective description

increased, decreased, rose, fell, peaked, remained constant, was unchanged, was higher than, was lower than, was identical to, did not occur, was absent, varied with. Pair with exact values.

## Evaluative modifiers (use for a stated reason, by strength)

- Large: markedly, substantially, considerably, strikingly, dramatically.
- Moderate: noticeably, appreciably, clearly, fairly.
- Small: slightly, marginally, somewhat, barely.
- Close to a target: nearly, virtually, essentially, approximately.
- Frame a number: "as many as 23%" reads as a lot, "only 23%" reads as little. Choose the frame that the benchmark supports.

## Comparing with others or with a model

consistent with, in line with, in good agreement with, comparable to, similar to, confirm, support, corroborate, validate, differ from, contrary to, inconsistent with, contradict. Strength differs: "consistent with" is weaker than "confirm". Put the citation next to the study compared.

## Problems

minor, localised, within the range of, not significant, unexpected, difficult to control, possible sources of error include. Pair each with size and a reason or next step.

## Implications (weak to strong)

may indicate, could reflect, suggests, indicates, is associated with, implies, provides evidence for. Reserve "demonstrates" and "shows" for results the design can support.

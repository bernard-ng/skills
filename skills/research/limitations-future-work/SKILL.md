# Limitations and future work

Handled well, limitations raise a reader's trust: they show the author knows the work's boundaries. Concealed problems that readers notice damage credibility, and limitations that first appear in the closing paragraphs look like an afterthought.

## Where each belongs

- **Methods:** problems with procedure, materials, equipment, sampling, or software, and their effect.
- **Results:** incomplete, anomalous, or unexpected results, with size and likely reason.
- **Discussion or Conclusion:** only those limitations that point to concrete future work, not ones inherent to the whole field.

Do not wait for perfect data. Write up work that is worth communicating and report its flaws honestly.

## Procedure

1. List every problem, deviation, and boundary condition with its source.
2. For each, state the facts and size: how many samples, which interval, what range.
3. State the effect on the results, and only call it small, minor, or negligible if you can show that it is.
4. Place a positive outcome that still stands near the limitation ("Although the hypothesis was supported, retention was not reassessed").
5. Offer a reason or a remedy: how it was handled, or what should change next time.
6. Turn the limitation into a next step. "Should" invites the community ("Retention should be tested over longer follow-up"). "Will" announces your own plan ("We will extend the follow-up to 12 months").
7. Prefer limitations that a feasible study could address.

## Guardrails

Minimising wording must not misstate the evidence. No minimisers such as "slightly", "only approximate", or "negligible" without support. Do not move limitations to the Discussion to avoid mentioning them earlier. Do not claim planned work you are not pursuing.

## Check

Is each problem reported where it arose? Does the wording match its true size? Does each Discussion limitation lead to a specific, feasible next step?

**Illustrative example:** "Two of 18 samples briefly contacted plastic tubing; their values differed from the rest by less than the measurement error (Table 2), so they were retained."

# Adverbs and modifier placement

An adverb can attach to an unintended word without breaking the grammar. These errors are easy to make and hard to detect.

## Procedure

1. Identify what each modifier is meant to modify: a verb, an adjective, a noun, or the whole sentence.
2. Read the sentence for the most plausible wrong attachment. Common traps: "with" (using or having), "after" and "during" (when or which event), "only", "nearly", "previously", "directly", "respectively", "using".
3. Move the modifier next to its target. A modifier that applies to the whole sentence (time, a result link) can go at the front.
4. Avoid clusters of adverbs and phrases. Split the sentence into units, each with its own modifier.
5. Re-read the result to check that the claim is unchanged.

## Guardrails

Do not move a modifier without confirming the intended scope; a grammatical sentence can still state the wrong method or outcome.

## Check

Could the reader mistake instrument for condition, cause for timing, or one subgroup for all groups?

**Illustrative example:** "Only the treated samples were heated" excludes heating the controls. "The treated samples were heated only for five minutes" limits the duration. A sentence with "in the lab last week with a new probe" is better split: "Last week we tested the sample in the lab; we used a new probe."

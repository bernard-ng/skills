# Articles and determiners

Article errors are hard to see and can change meaning.

## Procedure

1. Decide the noun's status in this use: singular countable, plural, or mass. Many nouns usually treated as mass can be countable with a different meaning (industries, steels, evidence of types, analyses).
2. A singular countable noun needs a determiner (a, the, this, each, one).
3. Use **the** when the writer and the reader both know which one is meant. That includes a previously mentioned item, something implied by what was mentioned (a device, then "the display"), something unique ("the second layer", "the centre of the mesh"), and a shared setting.
4. Use **a/an** when any member will do, or when the reader does not need to know which one. Use zero article for plural or mass items in general.
5. Check that the choice does not change the claim:
   - "This effect may hide a link between the variables" suggests a possible link.
   - "This effect may hide the link between the variables" assumes the link exists.
   - "Attach the nodes to two adjacent sites" means any two sites; "...to the two adjacent sites" means there are only two.
6. All three forms can express a general truth: "A sensor measures...", "The sensor measures...", "Sensors measure...". Choose the one that fits the register.
7. Choose a or an by sound: a UV lamp, an MRI scan, an X-ray.

## Guardrails

Do not decide solely by first mention, or by a fixed list of uncountable nouns. Do not use "the" if the reader cannot point to one referent.

## Check

Could a reader point to the entity "the" means? Does "a" accidentally make a specific known item sound arbitrary? Does the choice assert uniqueness or existence that the study did not establish?

**Illustrative example:** "A sensor was mounted on each pole. The sensor on pole 3 failed." The second phrase identifies one sensor.

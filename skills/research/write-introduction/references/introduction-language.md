# Introduction language bank

Functional wording to adapt, not sentences to copy. Check each choice against the target journal and the actual evidence.

## Establishing importance

- Amount of attention: "X has attracted considerable interest", "much recent work has focused on", "X has been studied extensively".
- Usefulness: "X plays a central role in", "X is widely used for", "X offers practical advantages", "X has potential applications in".
- Prevalence or growth: "an increasing number of", "X is now routine in", "X is a leading cause of".
- Accepted fact: "It is well established that", "X is generally regarded as".
- Tense: present perfect for amount of recent work; present simple for accepted facts and importance.

## Reporting prior work (choose the verb that matches what the researchers did)

| What they did | Verbs |
| --- | --- |
| Measured or observed | measured, monitored, detected, recorded, observed, identified |
| Calculated or modelled | calculated, estimated, modelled, simulated, predicted |
| Built or proposed | developed, designed, proposed, introduced, constructed, formulated |
| Established strongly | demonstrated, established, confirmed, showed |
| Established tentatively | suggested, indicated, reported, proposed |
| Took a position | argued, claimed, assumed, maintained |
| Tried | attempted, sought to, explored |

Use the verb that matches the source's strength and design. Do not upgrade "suggested" to "showed", and avoid "proved" for empirical findings. Past simple for a single completed study; present perfect for a body of work; present simple when the claim is treated as settled.

## Signalling and stating a gap

- Early signals: however, although, despite, yet, nevertheless, while.
- Absence: "little attention has been paid to", "few studies have examined", "X remains unclear", "no consistent evidence exists for X in Y".
- Limitation of earlier work: "these methods are time-consuming", "this approach has been limited to", "the assumption may not hold when".
- Prediction or hypothesis: "We therefore hypothesised that", "It was predicted that".
- "Few" and "little" mean almost none; "a few" and "a little" mean some. Choose deliberately, and support absence claims with a search.

## Softening criticism

Prefer impersonal, bounded wording: "may not always be fully effective" over "ineffective"; "has not yet been tested in" over "failed to consider"; "is limited to" over "is poor". Criticise the method or evidence, not the researcher.

## Describing the present paper

- Purpose: "The aim of this study was to", "This paper reports", "Here we test".
- Method: "Using X, we", "On the basis of these criteria, the paper then describes".
- Structure: "The paper is organised as follows". In a thesis, state the specific job of each chapter instead of the vague "discusses".
- Contribution: use "novel" or "first" only when supported.

# Quantity and comparison

**Inputs:** exact values, units, denominators, comparator, direction, uncertainty, and any statistical result.

## Procedure

1. State the measurable claim with the number first: value, unit, comparator, time, and direction.
2. Pair "higher", "lower", and "more than" with a named comparator.
3. Choose quantity wording by what it does:
   - **Enlarge:** a considerable amount, most, as many as, upwards of, at least.
   - **Reduce:** few, little, only, barely, hardly, marginal, negligible.
   - **Intensify:** far higher, well below, even greater, by far.
   - **Approximate:** nearly, approximately, virtually, practically, just under.
   - **Stay neutral:** some, relatively, fairly, moderate, to some extent.
4. Distinguish word pairs: "few" means almost none, "a few" means some; "little" means almost none, "a little" means some; "fewer" for countable items, "less" for amounts; absolute change versus percentage change versus percentage points.
5. Reserve "significant" for statistical significance and give the test or interval, or use another word.
6. Check arithmetic and units. Use a space between number and unit symbol, and the same unit throughout a comparison. Use forms such as "half the width of" and "twice as long as" consistently. See [conventions](../scientific-conventions/SKILL.md).
7. Decide whether to add interpretation, and which framing the benchmark supports. See [evaluative language](../evaluative-language/SKILL.md).

## Guardrails

Do not use "slight", "substantial", "negligible", or "approximately" to obscure an exact supplied number or an important exception. Do not infer practical importance from a p-value alone. Do not replace a known value with a vague quantity word.

## Check

Can a reader recover how much, compared with what, at what time, and in which direction?

**Illustrative example:** "Mean yield was 14% higher than the control (57% versus 50%)" mixes relative change with percentage points; verify the arithmetic and state which one is meant ("7 percentage points, a 14% relative increase").

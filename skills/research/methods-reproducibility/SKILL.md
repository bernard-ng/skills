# Methods reproducibility

**Inputs:** the actual protocol, lab notes or data dictionary, design, sampling, instruments, analysis plan or code, and known deviations.

## Procedure

1. Reconstruct the workflow in ordered stages and compare each stage with the text. Use the [Methodology](../write-methodology/SKILL.md) model for placement.
2. Check eligibility and sampling, controls, materials and their sources, equipment and settings, quantities and units, timing, measurements, exclusions, missing data, analysis, and approvals where relevant.
3. Audit vague wording: "various", "some", "standard", "appropriate", "for a short time". Each needs a value, a citation, or both.
4. Audit attribution. For each procedure decide whether it is standard, cited, adapted, or this study's own, and check that tense, wording, and citation placement say which. See [voice](../active-passive-voice/SKILL.md) and [citation attribution](../citation-attribution/SKILL.md).
5. Audit justification. Each non-obvious choice (site, material, instrument, sample size, model) should carry a reason, so readers can accept it.
6. Audit care claims. Words like "carefully" or "thoroughly" should describe something that was done and, if possible, how.
7. Audit problems. Equipment limits, contamination risks, small samples, and preliminary trials that changed the design belong here, with their effect. See [limitations and future work](../limitations-future-work/SKILL.md).
8. Check conventions: SI symbols and spacing, consistent units, defined abbreviations. See [conventions](../scientific-conventions/SKILL.md). For apparatus geometry, see [spatial description](../spatial-description/SKILL.md).
9. Separate planned procedure from departures. Check text, tables, and supplements for conflicts, and whether details moved to Results are cross-referenced.
10. Return a short question list for details the author must supply. Edit only from verified information.

## Check

A competent reader should be able to evaluate the design and repeat the reported procedure at the journal's expected level. Do not fill gaps with plausible values or silently remove deviations.

**Illustrative issue:** "Samples were heated" needs a temperature, duration, equipment, and atmosphere if those affect the result.

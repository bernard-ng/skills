# Signalling language

The space between one sentence and the next is where readers get lost. Close it deliberately.

## Four ways to connect sentences

1. **Overlap.** Repeat a key term from the previous sentence.
2. **Pronoun or pro-form.** "It", "they", "this method", "these systems", "this combination". Make sure the referent is unmistakable.
3. **Join.** Use a semicolon when two short sentences are closely related, or a relative clause ("which...").
4. **Connector.** Name the relation, then choose a connector.

## Relations and connectors

| Relation | Examples | Cautions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cause | because, as, since, due to, owing to, in view of | "Since" can mean time; "due to" needs a noun |
| Result | therefore, consequently, hence, as a result | "So" at the start of a sentence is informal |
| Contrast | however, whereas, while, by contrast, but | "While" can mean at the same time; "conversely" and "on the contrary" mean the exact opposite, not just a difference |
| Unexpected | although, even though, despite, nevertheless, yet | Needs a result contrary to expectation |
| Addition | in addition, furthermore, moreover, also | "Besides" is more persuasive and informal |
| Example | for example, in particular | Give a real example |
| Sequence | first, then, subsequently | See [sequence](../sequence-language/SKILL.md) |

## Procedure

1. Name the relation between the two propositions before choosing a connector.
2. Put the signal early in the sentence ("Although X, Y") so the reader knows the function before reading the detail.
3. Check the grammar after the connector: "although" and "despite" take different structures.
4. Use a new paragraph and an early contrast to mark an important or unexpected point (see [paragraphing](../paragraph-coherence/SKILL.md)).
5. Vary connectors and avoid a long run of "however" that turns the section into a point-counterpoint exchange. Group related studies or results instead.

## Guardrails

Do not add connectors just to sound academic. "Therefore" requires a warranted consequence; "however" requires a real contrast. When "since" or "while" could be read two ways, choose an unambiguous alternative.

## Check

Could a reader draw the intended link without guessing? Is the link itself supported by the evidence?

**Illustrative example:** "The sensors were calibrated indoors. However, their response changed after outdoor exposure." The connector marks a real contrast.

# Vocabulary selection

**Inputs:** the intended rhetorical job, field, sample articles, and the exact scientific meaning.

Scientific writing uses a fairly small set of non-technical words as a kind of code (attempt, conduct, determine, evaluate, investigate, interpret, enhance), plus field terms. Learn the functional vocabulary, keep technical terms exact.

## Procedure

1. Choose words by function before synonym: establish importance, report an aim, describe a procedure, locate a gap, point to a display, compare findings, qualify a limitation, state an implication.
2. Replace generic verbs with specific ones. Instead of "did" or "was done", use what was done (calibrated, extracted, filtered). Instead of "showed" for prior work, use what the researchers did (measured, modelled, proposed, compared).
3. Match verb strength to the evidence: suggest, indicate, show, demonstrate. Do not upgrade.
4. Keep formal register. Avoid informal connectors ("so", "also" at the start), contractions, and exclamation marks. Express excitement with precise strong words (striking, remarkable, unexpected) used sparingly and only when the data justify it.
5. Soften criticism of prior work with impersonal wording about the method or scope.
6. Check collocations and prepositions. If unsure, look at how recent target articles use the verb with the noun, then write your own sentence.
7. Check noun, infinitive, and -ing forms in justification phrases ("to reduce", "reducing", "the reduction of").
8. Keep each technical term consistent across the whole paper. Do not rotate synonyms for variety.
9. Build a short phrase bank from sample articles, organised by function and grammar. See [target article analysis](../target-article-analysis/SKILL.md) and the phrase banks in the section skills: [Introduction](../write-introduction/references/introduction-language.md), [Methods](../write-methodology/references/methods-language.md), [Results](../write-results/references/results-language.md), [Discussion](../write-discussion-conclusion/references/discussion-language.md).

## Guardrails

Do not swap technical near-synonyms to avoid repetition. Avoid fashionable or inflated words where a plain precise word works. Do not fabricate novelty or strength adjectives.

## Check

Is each term conventional in the field, exact in meaning, and consistent throughout? Does any evaluative word imply more than the data?

**Illustrative example:** "We evaluated sensor drift" names a procedure; "drift was detected" reports an observation; "drift may limit deployment length" states an implication. These are not interchangeable.

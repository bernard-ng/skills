# Paper consistency audit

Obtain the manuscript, tables and figures, source data if available, bibliography, and target journal requirements.

## Procedure

1. Build a trace table: each aim or hypothesis, its method, its result, and its conclusion. Flag missing or unsupported links.
2. Check the section shape. The Introduction should narrow to the aim; the Discussion should widen back, revisit the aim, and map the study onto the research described earlier; the Abstract and Title should predict the body.
3. Compare sample sizes, group names, time points, units, statistical terms, labels, abbreviations, and terminology throughout.
4. Check problems and limitations. Each should be mentioned where it arose (Methods, Results) and not first appear in the Discussion. See [limitations and future work](../limitations-future-work/SKILL.md).
5. Check evidence strength. Discussion claims, Abstract claims, and Title wording must not exceed the Results or design. See [claim strength](../claim-strength/SKILL.md).
6. Check that wording echoes across sections: the same aim, term, and gap statement in Introduction, Results, and Discussion.
7. Check attribution, tense, and voice consistency for cited versus own work.
8. Run [style editing](../scientific-style-editing/SKILL.md) after substantive inconsistencies are resolved. Report findings by location, evidence, consequence, and a concrete fix or author question.

## Check

Every major claim traces to a result; every major result relates to a stated question or is marked exploratory. List facts that cannot be checked from the supplied material. Do not silently harmonise conflicting numbers.

**Illustrative issue:** The Abstract says 120 samples while Methods says 118 analysed. Identify the discrepancy and request the documented exclusion count.

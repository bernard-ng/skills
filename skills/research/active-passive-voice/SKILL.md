# Active and passive voice

**Inputs:** who performed each action, the journal's or institution's author-voice rule, and whether the text is an article or a single-author thesis.

## Procedure

1. Check the author guide or supervisor for voice rules before choosing.
2. Use **active** when the actor matters or attribution could blur: "We measured..." or "Chen et al. measured...". Use **passive** when the object or procedure is the natural topic: "Temperature was recorded hourly."
3. Remember the cost of the agentless passive: the reader cannot tell who acted. Mark ownership with a phrase ("In this study", "In our experiments"), a citation ("In Brown (1999)"), a dummy subject ("This experiment", "The procedure"), or the active voice.
4. Use "we" for the author team only. For people in general, use "It is known that...", not "We know that...".
5. In a single-author thesis, use passive, "this thesis", "here", "in this study", or a dummy subject rather than "I".
6. In the Introduction, a dummy subject ("The present paper presents...") is often clearer than "is presented".
7. Apply the cases below in Methods.

## Methods cases

| What you mean | Make it clear by |
| --- | --- |
| This study's own action | Past tense plus "here", "in this work", the active voice, or a dummy subject |
| A cited author's action | A citation or "in their work", with a tense that fits |
| A standard procedure or equipment | Present tense, with a citation if not universal |
| A cited fact versus own step in a row | Keep tenses distinct, then return to past when you stop describing the standard |
| A field that requires present tense for own procedure | Add "here", "in this model", or the active voice |

Without a tense or attribution difference, a reader may credit your key innovation to a cited method, or a cited method to you.

## Guardrails

Do not ban either voice. Do not use "we" for humanity. Never let an agentless passive credit this study with previous work. Check citation placement when reporting a cited procedure.

## Check

At each passive, can a reader tell whether it refers to this study, another study, or a standard method?

**Illustrative example:** "In the earlier study, samples were frozen before analysis. Here, samples were analysed within two hours of collection."

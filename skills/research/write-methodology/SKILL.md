# Methodology

**Inputs:** design and purpose, materials and sources, sample selection, apparatus and settings, quantities, times, places, controls, analysis, approvals where relevant, deviations, and known problems.

The section must let a competent reader repeat the work **and** accept it. Readers who doubt the methods will doubt the results and conclusions, so the section records and also persuades: this is exactly what was done, and there were good reasons.

## Four-part menu

Select what suits the study and journal. A narrow, familiar method may start straight at the procedure.

1. **Orient the reader.** A short overview of the parameters (number of tests, equipment, software, purpose), or the source and background of materials, or a link back to the aim. Give the general picture before the details, and repeat the overview at the start of each subsection.
2. **Give precise, ordered details.** Quantities, temperatures, durations, sequence, conditions, locations, sizes, and controls. Add the reason for each non-obvious choice (a property, a validation by earlier work, a capability). Signal care where it is true (carefully, thoroughly, separately, in triplicate).
3. **Relate to other work.** Mark each procedure as identical to, adapted from, or different from a cited method. Give the key details even for a cited method, because readers should not need a library trip. A real difference may be the paper's contribution.
4. **Report problems.** Say what went wrong or was imperfect, how large the effect was, and what was done. Omit this move if nothing applies. See [limitations and future work](../limitations-future-work/SKILL.md).

## Procedure

- Map the actual workflow first. Group by experiment or analytic stage, then order the steps within each stage. See [sequence](../sequence-language/SKILL.md).
- Keep standard procedure separate from your own work using tense and attribution. See [voice](../active-passive-voice/SKILL.md) and [tense](../tense-selection/SKILL.md). Present passive describes what is normally done or how equipment is built; past passive describes what was done in this study.
- Place citations beside the part of the sentence they support, so you do not credit others with your work or the reverse. See [citation attribution](../citation-attribution/SKILL.md).
- Use SI symbols and consistent units. See [conventions](../scientific-conventions/SKILL.md). Describe apparatus layout with [spatial description](../spatial-description/SKILL.md).
- Replace vague verbs ("done", "various", "some") with specific ones and exact values. See [phrase bank](references/methods-language.md).
- Do not analyse or comment on the effects of the procedure here. Mention a result only if preliminary results shaped the design. When the journal keeps only the basic framework here, fuller method detail may sit in Results beside the findings it explains.
- Check [articles](../articles-determiners/SKILL.md) and [modifier placement](../modifier-placement/SKILL.md).

## Guardrails

Do not invent or "clean up" procedures, settings, exclusion rules, or approvals. Preserve deviations that affect interpretation. Do not claim care that the record does not support.

## Check

Could a competent reader reconstruct the design, distinguish planned steps from deviations, and see why each important choice was made? Is every adaptation attributed? Is each source, quantity, condition, and analysis explicit? Are problems stated here rather than first appearing in the Discussion?

**Illustrative example:** "For this study, sensors were placed at three roadside locations for 14 days. Readings were recorded every 10 minutes and compared with the station monitor, because the station is the regulatory reference; intervals with power loss were excluded."

# Contribution mapping

The Introduction drew a research map. The Discussion shows where this study sits on it, what it adds, and what follows.

## Procedure

1. List the studies, theories, or predictions your results touch. Use those introduced earlier; adding many new studies at this stage is unusual.
2. For each, decide the relation: confirms, extends, refines, qualifies, contradicts, reaches similar results by a different method, or is the first of its kind. Use the verb that matches the evidence ([phrase bank](../write-discussion-conclusion/references/discussion-language.md)).
3. State the achievement separately in positive, explicit terms: what was accomplished, the benefit, and the scope. Keep wording to what the evidence earns. See [evaluative language](../evaluative-language/SKILL.md).
4. State the implication, and explain it. Say why the result occurred (a mechanism, a reason), how it answers the original aim or gap, whether another interpretation fits, and why an unexpected result may have arisen. Do not restate the result in new words.
5. Check novelty claims. Search thoroughly before claiming "first"; even then, "to our knowledge" covers residual risk.
6. Add applications only if the study has them. Look first at the opening of the Introduction and similar papers to see how the work is used, and use modals such as "could" or "may" unless the use was tested.
7. Calibrate strength with [claim strength](../claim-strength/SKILL.md), and keep it consistent with the Abstract and Conclusion.
8. Remember tense. Present perfect or present simple states achievements ("We have shown", "This study provides").

## Guardrails

Do not claim priority, generality, or application beyond what the design supports. Do not treat agreement with one study as proof. Do not attack other work: describe differences in method, scope, or evidence.

## Check

Can the reader see where the work fits, what is new, and how far the finding reaches? Do the claims match the Results and design?

**Illustrative example:** "These findings extend earlier laboratory calibration studies to season-long outdoor use at three roadside sites, and suggest that recalibration could shorten required deployment checks."

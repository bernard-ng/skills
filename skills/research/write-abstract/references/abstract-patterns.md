# Abstract patterns

## Tense by component

| Component | Typical tense | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gap or problem | Present simple | "However, this assumption does not hold when..." |
| What the paper does | Present simple | "This paper reports...", "We present..." |
| Aim | Past or present | Past when describing what the study set out to do |
| Method | Past simple, sometimes present for equations or numerical procedures | "Samples were prepared..." |
| Result | Past simple, or present when treated as established | Present can follow a past reporting verb: "We found that X increases with Y" |
| Achievement | Present perfect or present simple | "We have developed..." |
| Application | Present simple, often with can or may | "This approach can be applied to..." |
| Limitation or future work | Present or modal | Rare and brief |

## Sentence-combining patterns (original, illustrative)

- Gap plus achievement: "Unlike cost-based planning, the proposed model also scores supply quality."
- Achievement plus method: "Yield rose after in-line filtration was added."
- Problem plus method: "To find the best option under several objectives, a decision tool based on life-cycle assessment was used."
- Aim plus method: "To test X, we did Y."
- Method plus result: "Across 40 trials, the new protocol gave a release rate of 10 to 20% per hour."

## Content checks

- Results: give at least one number tied to a benchmark where available.
- Method: name the approach if a reader needs it to decide whether to read (simulation, prototype, field data, survey).
- Implication: consistent with the paper's Discussion; "may" or "could" only when it is truly uncertain.
- Language: slightly less specialised than the body; define unusual terms and acronyms once.
- Keywords: put search phrases in the text itself, naturally.

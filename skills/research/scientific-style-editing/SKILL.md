# Revision and checking

**Inputs:** the draft, source data and tables, cited references, target journal instructions, and tracked author decisions.

## Four passes

1. **Structure.** Label each paragraph with its move. Check that the Introduction narrows to the aim, Methods lets a reader assess or repeat the work, Results trace the evidence with interpretation, the Discussion answers the aim and widens, the Abstract reflects the paper, and the Title predicts its scope. Run the skim test: read only the first sentence of each paragraph.
2. **Evidence and meaning.** Verify every number, unit, denominator, figure reference, citation, method, limitation, and priority claim against the supplied source. Separate this study from cited work and observation from interpretation. Check that problems appear where they arose. Flag missing evidence rather than filling it in.
3. **Language.** Check tense shifts, passive agents, pronoun referents, connectors, articles, modifier scope, sequence, frequency, quantity, causality, modals, and paragraph transitions. Read sentences as a sceptical reader who does not know the intended meaning, hunting for invisible errors.
4. **Conventions.** Check units, abbreviations, plurals, capitalisation, reference style, headings, word limits, and journal rules. See [conventions](../scientific-conventions/SKILL.md).

The language passes use these skills: [tense](../tense-selection/SKILL.md), [voice](../active-passive-voice/SKILL.md), [signalling](../signalling-transitions/SKILL.md), [paragraphing](../paragraph-coherence/SKILL.md), [articles](../articles-determiners/SKILL.md), [modifiers](../modifier-placement/SKILL.md), [noun phrases](../noun-phrases-prepositions/SKILL.md), [sequence](../sequence-language/SKILL.md), [frequency](../frequency-language/SKILL.md), [quantity](../quantity-comparisons/SKILL.md), [causality](../causal-language/SKILL.md), [modal verbs](../modality/SKILL.md), [claim strength](../claim-strength/SKILL.md), [vocabulary](../academic-vocabulary/SKILL.md).

## Output

Return a clean revision when asked, plus a short change log for edits that affect meaning and a list of unresolved questions. If asked only to diagnose, prioritise problems that change scientific meaning over surface fluency.

**Guardrail:** Never silently alter data, references, or degree of certainty. Preserve the author's voice where it does not obstruct clarity.

**Example audit note:** "The draft calls this a causal effect, but the supplied design is observational. I changed 'caused' to 'was associated with'; please confirm this matches the analysis."

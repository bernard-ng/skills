# Causality

**Inputs:** the design, intervention or exposure, confounder control, temporal order, mechanistic evidence, and alternatives.

## Procedure

1. Identify what the evidence shows: an intervention effect, a supported mechanism, or only an association.
2. Choose the verb by strength.

| Strength | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Direct cause | caused, produced, brought about, resulted in, was due to, generated |
| Partial cause | contributed to, was a factor in, influenced, affected |
| First step in a chain | initiated, triggered, originated in |
| Consequence | resulted from, arose from, stemmed from, was a consequence of |
| Association only | was associated with, was linked to, was related to, accompanied, correlated with |

3. Check direction: "X resulted in Y" makes X the cause, "X resulted from Y" makes Y the cause. "A cause of" means one of several; "the cause of" means the only one.
4. If causality is weak, report the relation and frame the mechanism as a hypothesis. Distinguish sequence ("after"), covariance ("associated with"), and cause.
5. If you must soften, choose bounded frames ("The evidence suggests that", "It appears that", "There is evidence to indicate that"), a frequency or quantity limit ("in some cases", "often"), or a modal ("may have caused"). See [modal verbs](../modality/SKILL.md) and [claim strength](../claim-strength/SKILL.md).

## Guardrails

No connector or hedge can repair a false causal inference. Do not stack hedges until the sentence says nothing ("The evidence points to the possibility that in many cases x can contribute to some types of y").

## Check

What result would challenge the proposed mechanism? Does the design justify the verb chosen?

**Illustrative example:** Observational data: "Higher traffic volume was associated with higher particle counts." Controlled intervention, if supported: "Closing the intake reduced particle counts."

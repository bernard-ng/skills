# Modal verbs

Modal verbs adjust how true or certain a statement sounds. Many have more than one meaning, and they change in the past and in negatives.

## Procedure

Name the function before choosing the modal.

| Function | Present | Past or perfect | Negative |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ability | can | could (general past ability), was able to (a specific past success) | cannot, could not |
| Possibility | may, might, could, can | may have, might have, could have | may not, might not (possibly not) |
| Impossibility | cannot | cannot have, could not have | |
| Probability or expectation | should, ought to | should have | should not |
| Near-certain inference | must, has to | must have | cannot (impossible) |
| Advice | should | should have (it was not done) | should not |
| Necessity | must, needs to, has to | needed to, had to | need not, does not need to |

## Key distinctions

- **May not** means possibly not; **cannot** means impossible. "Can not" is rare and means possibly not.
- **Must** for inference signals near-certainty without direct proof. If you have direct evidence, state it plainly.
- **Must not** means prohibited; **need not** or **does not have to** means unnecessary. "We did not need to heat the valves" does not say whether they were heated; "We need not have heated them" implies that they were.
- **Could** can mean possibility or past ability. Use **was able to** for a specific past success.
- **Should** can mean probability or advice; rewrite if ambiguous.
- For future work, "should be tested" invites others; "will test" announces your own plan.
- "May well" strengthens possibility; "might" is slightly weaker than "may".
- **Can** has no other tenses: use "be able to" or "be capable of" for future or perfect forms.

## Guardrails

Journal style and field meaning govern the final choice. "Must" is rarely justified by one noisy result. Do not use modals to hide a stated finding.

## Check

Could the reader mistake permission for possibility, advice for inference, or logical necessity for a methodological requirement?

**Illustrative example:** "The sensor may have drifted during storage" (possible past explanation). "The sensor must be recalibrated before use" (requirement). "The leak cannot be the cause" (ruled out).

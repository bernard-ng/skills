# Citation attribution

**Inputs:** the draft and the bibliography or source documents. If sources are unavailable, mark the citations for verification.

## Procedure

1. Extract every claim about prior work, consensus, novelty, or a borrowed method and identify the source it needs.
2. Decide whether a citation is needed:
   - Background facts that come from research: yes.
   - A recent or specialised problem: yes.
   - A very well-known problem or fact: usually not.
   - A standard method: depends on how familiar it is to the readership; cite when in doubt.
   - Prior work referred to by name, comparison, or "as reported": always.
3. Check that the source supports the exact wording, population, design, and time scope. Distinguish the source's finding from this paper's interpretation.
4. Place the citation after the part of the sentence it supports, not automatically at the end. A mixed sentence may need mid-sentence citations. Superscript or numbered citations should cover all and only the work referred to.
5. In Methods, make sure a passive sentence does not credit this study with a cited procedure, or the reverse. Add "in this study", "in X (year)", or a citation. See [voice](../active-passive-voice/SKILL.md).
6. Match the reporting verb to the source's strength and design. See the [verb bank](../write-introduction/references/introduction-language.md).
7. Flag missing sources, mismatched attribution, "first" or "no studies" claims, and quotations needing exact verification. Check that "to our knowledge" is backed by a real search. Prefer paraphrase and synthesis to quotation, and credit the ideas, not only the words.
8. Expect few citations in an abstract, and few first-time studies in the Discussion. Apply [literature positioning](../literature-review-positioning/SKILL.md) where synthesis is weak.

## Check

A reader should be able to trace each external claim to its source. Never create bibliographic entries or infer a source's contents from its title or abstract alone.

**Illustrative issue:** "Smith et al. proved the mechanism" needs a source that tested the mechanism, and wording calibrated to its design.

## What it does

Builds an Introduction in four moves: establish importance and background, situate prior research, locate a gap, describe the present paper.

It narrows from the field to the study, opens with something every reader accepts rather than with the problem, and never invents a gap or a citation.

## When to reach for it

- **Invocation.** Model-invoked: the agent reaches for it when the task fits, or you can name it.
- **Trigger boundary.** Reach for this for any opening background, gap, or aim text. For just the literature part use [literature-review-positioning](./literature-review-positioning.md); for just the gap, [formulate-research-gap](./formulate-research-gap.md).

## Common questions

**Why not start with my problem?**
Readers need background to judge the problem, and a one-sentence problem statement tends to sprawl. Start general.

**How much background is enough?**
It depends on how broad the readership is. When unsure, a little more than less, kept proportional to the question.

## It's working if

- The first sentence is something all readers accept.
- The gap sentence signals its function early (However, Although).
- The last sentences state what this paper does.

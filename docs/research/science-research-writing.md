## What it does

The router for all research writing. It classifies the task, picks the section and language skills, sets the drafting order, and holds the table of textual signals that should fire each specialist.

It writes no facts of its own: it works only from the data, methods, and references you supply, and marks gaps instead of filling them.

## When to reach for it

- **Invocation.** Model-invoked: the agent reaches for it when the task fits, or you can name it.
- **Trigger boundary.** Reach for this on any request to plan, draft, restructure, review, or polish research prose, even a vague "improve this paragraph". For a single narrow decision, go straight to the specialist, for example [tense-selection](./tense-selection.md).

## Common questions

**Do I have to name a section?**
No. It infers the section from the content: aims and background suggest an Introduction, "we measured" suggests Methods, numbers suggest Results.

**Why does it draft Methods before the Introduction?**
The Introduction has to describe the study that was actually done, and the Abstract and Title derive from the finished paper. Presentation order is unchanged.

**Does it work for a thesis?**
Yes, with the voice and literature-review adjustments in its Theses section. Your institution's rules win.

## It's working if

- Each section answers how it starts, what goes in what order, and how it ends.
- A short list of factual gaps accompanies every draft.
- Meaning-affecting edits come with a change log.
